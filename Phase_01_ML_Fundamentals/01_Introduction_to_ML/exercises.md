# Exercises: Introduction to Machine Learning

### Beginner Exercises

**Exercise 1: Supervised vs Unsupervised**
Identify whether the following scenarios require Supervised or Unsupervised Learning:
1. Predicting house prices based on square footage and location.
2. Grouping a supermarket's customers into 5 distinct categories based on purchasing behavior.
3. Teaching a computer to play chess by rewarding it for winning.
4. Detecting whether an image contains a dog or a cat.

**Exercise 2: Traditional vs ML**
Write a short paragraph explaining why writing a traditional `if/else` program to transcribe human speech to text is practically impossible, and why Machine Learning is required.

### Intermediate Exercises

**Exercise 3: The ML Pipeline**
In `practice.py`, we implemented a basic ML pipeline. Add a new feature to the mock dataset `X` (for example, "number of spelling mistakes"). 
1. Update the `X` array to have 4 columns.
2. Retrain the model and see if it runs successfully.

**Exercise 4: Data Collection**
Think of a real-world problem you want to solve with AI (e.g., predicting flight delays). 
List out exactly:
- What your Features ($X$) would be.
- What your Target ($y$) would be.
- Where you could realistically collect this data.

### Advanced Exercises

**Exercise 5: Mathematical Intuition (Research)**
Look up the equation for a Straight Line: $y = mx + c$.
In Machine Learning, this is written as $\hat{y} = w_1x_1 + b$.
Explain in your own words what $w$ (weight) and $b$ (bias) represent in the context of predicting a salary based on years of experience.
